\begin{abstract}
Benchmarks for single-cell clustering commonly fix each method's setting using the
reference labels, either by supplying the true number of cell types or by keeping the run
that reproduces it. The scores that result are conditional on information a user does not
have. We ask what happens to one such published advantage, that of the mutual information
clustering MICA in scMINER over a principal component and Leiden pipeline, when the same
label-free rule chooses the setting for both methods. We apply four rules to nine
datasets and the main rule to a tenth and much larger one. The main rule is subsampling
stability in the style of chooseR, reduced to 25 subsamples per setting from the 100 it
specifies and repeated at three seeds. Across the nine, MICA leads under three of the four
rules, but by amounts the rule rather than the method decides.
Its mean adjusted Rand index ranges from 0.473 to 0.727 across rules, a spread of 0.255,
against 0.120 for the baseline. Two readings of the same stability scores reverse the
ranking, MICA leading 0.661 to 0.632 under the published reading and trailing 0.473 to
0.635 under the other, a move of 0.189 for MICA against 0.003 for the baseline. The cause
is measurable: among settings the rule treats as equivalent, the span of cluster counts
averages 7.3 for MICA and 2.7 for the baseline, and on two datasets the rule cannot
separate 2 clusters from 20. MICA's partitions are too reproducible for reproducibility to
discriminate, so splitting a true type survives resampling as readily as recovering it.
The rule's tie-break, its threshold and its random draw each move the result by as much as
the gap between the methods. The effect is bounded. On the one dataset above 5,000 cells,
where MICA switches to its graph mode, the ambiguity disappears and MICA leads outright,
0.629 against 0.397. Running both of MICA's modes on one fixed pool of 2,500 cells, at
three seeds, shows that the mode and not the dataset size is what matters: on the same
cells the two readings of the rule disagree on every pool under small-data mode and on none
under graph mode. The failure therefore belongs to MICA's small-data pipeline, which nine of the ten
benchmark datasets use, rather than to mutual information clustering itself.
\end{abstract}
