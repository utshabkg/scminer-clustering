\begin{abstract}
Benchmarks of single-cell clustering usually fix each method's setting with the reference labels, so
their rankings assume information a user does not have. We ask how the candidate settings and the
label-free rule that chooses among them affect the reliability of such a comparison. The case is
scMINER's mutual information clustering, MICA, against principal components followed by Leiden or by
$k$-means on the ten scMINER benchmark datasets, with settings chosen by subsampling stability in the
style of chooseR. After exploratory runs at three seeds, we fixed a protocol in advance and ran seven new
seeds on the nine small datasets and five seeds, two of them new, on PBMC14K, scoring every run with our
implementation and with chooseR's own code. On the nine small datasets the candidates decide the
plain-maximum ranking: on each method's own grid Leiden leads MICA at every new seed,
\V{S0.original.M.leiden} against \V{S0.original.M.mica} in mean adjusted Rand index, and offered the same
cluster counts the two are level. The reading of the scores and the number of subsamples move the ranking
as well, and the bootstrap interval moves individual choices: when a coarse setting reproduces perfectly,
chooseR's code cannot form its interval and makes no choice, which happens in most MICA runs. The effects
of the candidates and of the reading met criteria fixed in advance, while those of the number of
subsamples and of the interval were measured without them. On matched candidates, MICA's lead under
chooseR's reading holds at every seed, but with 25 subsamples it rests on one dataset. We separate the
checks these findings suggest into six that run on unlabeled data and ask whether the menu is fair and
the choice determined by the data, and five that need labels and judge a benchmark's ranking. On Pollen,
flat stability scores and disagreeing readings show without labels that the data do not determine
MICA's count, and the labels confirm it: the two readings' picks score \V{L.pollen.mica.ariF} and
\V{L.pollen.mica.ariM} in median adjusted Rand index. On all \V{large.n_cells} cells of the Allen mouse brain atlas, both PCA pipelines
ran in \V{large.full.minutes_cached} minutes once the raw files were cached. No single process used
more than \V{large.max_process_gb}~GB, though the processes running side by side held up to \V{large.full.max_gb}~GB together. MICA would need about \V{large.mica.extrap_tb}~TB for the whole atlas, so it ran on a
100,000-cell sample and the other cells were assigned to its clusters.
\end{abstract}
