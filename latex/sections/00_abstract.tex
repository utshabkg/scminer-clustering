\begin{abstract}
Benchmarks for single-cell clustering commonly fix each method's setting using the
reference labels, either by supplying the true number of cell types or by keeping the run
that reproduces it. The scores that result are conditional on information a user does not
have. We ask what happens to one such published advantage, that of the mutual information
clustering MICA in scMINER over a principal component and Leiden pipeline, when the same
label-free rule chooses the setting for both methods. Applying four rules to nine datasets, including subsampling stability as specified by
chooseR with 25 subsamples per candidate setting and three seeds, we find that MICA
leads under three of the four, but by amounts the rule rather than the method decides.
Its mean adjusted Rand index ranges from 0.473 to 0.727 across rules, a spread of 0.255,
against 0.120 for the baseline. Two readings of the same stability scores reverse the
ranking, MICA leading 0.661 to 0.632 under the published reading and trailing 0.473 to
0.635 under the other, a move of 0.189 for MICA and 0.003 for the baseline. The cause is
measurable: among settings the rule treats as equivalent, the span of cluster counts
averages 7.3 for MICA and 2.7 for the baseline, and on one dataset the rule cannot
distinguish 3 clusters from 19. MICA's
partitions are too reproducible for reproducibility to discriminate, because its distance is
computed per cell pair and does not move when the sample changes, so splitting a true type
survives resampling as readily as recovering it. Three incidental choices inside the rule, its tie-break, its threshold and its random
draw, each move the result by as much as the gap between the methods. On these datasets
the uncertainty introduced by label-free selection exceeds the difference the benchmark
was reporting. The PBMC14K run in MICA's graph mode and a transfer rule remain to be completed.
\end{abstract}
