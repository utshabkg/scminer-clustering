\begin{abstract}
Benchmarks for single-cell clustering usually fix each method's setting with the reference labels, so
their rankings assume information a user does not have. We ask how a label-free rule changes such a
ranking, and when the result can be trusted. The case is scMINER's mutual information clustering, MICA,
against principal components followed by Leiden or by $k$-means on the ten scMINER benchmark datasets,
with settings chosen by subsampling stability in the style of chooseR. After exploratory runs at three
seeds, we fixed a protocol in advance and ran seven new seeds on the nine small datasets and seeds 0 to 4 on PBMC14K, scoring every run with our implementation and with chooseR's own code. The candidates decide the
plain-maximum ranking. On each method's own grid Leiden leads MICA on the nine small datasets at every
seed, \V{S0.original.M.leiden} against \V{S0.original.M.mica} in mean adjusted Rand index, and offered
the same cluster counts the two are level. The reading of the scores and the number of subsamples move
the ranking as well, and the bootstrap interval moves individual choices. Under chooseR's reading MICA
leads on matched candidates at every seed, but dataset by dataset the methods split about evenly, and
with 25 subsamples the mean lead rests on one dataset. When a coarse setting reproduces perfectly,
chooseR's code as written cannot form its interval and makes no choice, which happens in most MICA runs.
On PBMC14K the two tie on matched candidates under the plain maximum at four of five seeds, but the tie depends on which setting stands for each count, and on the original grids MICA leads. We turn these findings into six checks, none of which needs labels, that flag a comparison under label-free selection whose ranking should not be trusted.
\end{abstract}
