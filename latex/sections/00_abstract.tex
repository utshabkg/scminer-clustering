\begin{abstract}
Benchmarks for single-cell clustering usually fix each method's setting with the reference
labels, so their scores assume information a user does not have. We ask what happens to the
advantage scMINER reports for its mutual information clustering, MICA, over a principal
component and Leiden pipeline when one label-free rule chooses the setting for both. The rule
is subsampling stability in the style of chooseR, run at three seeds on all ten benchmark
datasets. Under a shared cap of 20 clusters, two readings of the same stability scores reverse
the ranking on the nine small datasets. The reversal comes from the candidates, because a cap
does not give the methods the same cluster counts. Offered the same counts, one setting each,
MICA leads under both readings at every seed, 0.662 against 0.543 and 0.473 against 0.452 in
mean adjusted Rand index. Its own ambiguity remains. Its stability scores are flat at the top,
mostly on datasets of a few hundred cells, and the choice of reading still moves it more than the
baseline. On PBMC14K, the one large benchmark dataset, the methods tie under one reading, and
under the other a 0.02 change in its threshold reverses which leads. We also compare a faster
neighbour graph, a correlation shortlist rescored exactly, with MICA's own search. It builds the
graph 24 times faster on PBMC14K and 2.2 times faster on 100,000 Allen cells, but needs three to
four times the memory to build and gives no better clustering.
\end{abstract}
