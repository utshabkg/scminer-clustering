\section{Conclusion and Remaining Work}\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked whether the advantage scMINER reports for mutual information clustering survives when
the method must choose its own clustering without reference labels. Under one published
label-free rule, applied identically to MICA and to a principal component and Leiden pipeline
on ten datasets at three seeds, it survives in direction on the nine small datasets once both
methods are offered the same candidates. There MICA leads under both readings of the rule at
every seed, and under the silhouette rule on the same candidates. With only a shared cap of 20
clusters, one reading reversed the ranking, so a cap is not enough. The size of the lead still
depends on the rule's tie-break, threshold and random draw, MICA's stability scores are flat at
the top on most of the smallest datasets, and its default of four clusters is what tuning on
this benchmark picks, so its strong default score is no support. On PBMC14K, the one large
benchmark dataset and the only one in graph mode, the plain maximum leaves the methods tied and
the threshold decides which leads, so that dataset supports no ranking.
These findings are about one rule, and we do not extend them to mutual information clustering
under other rules.

The faster neighbour graph is a faster graph rather than a better one. It gives the same
recall and clustering as MICA's on PBMC14K at a fraction of the build time, and on the Allen
cells higher recall in less than half the build time, without a better clustering. On both it needs
three to four times the memory.

\subsection*{Plan through November 18}
The main experiments are complete with this submission, and the full manuscript draft is due on
November 18. Two items remain, and each answers an open question above rather than adding
scope.

\textbf{A test of size on the same data.} MICA's flat top is widest on four of the five
smallest datasets, Buettner being the exception, but nine datasets cannot separate size from the datasets themselves. Drawing pools of 150 and
300 cells from Zeisel and from PBMC14K and rerunning the matched rule on them at three seeds
tests whether MICA's stability flattens when the same data are made small.

\textbf{The manuscript draft,} with the matched runs as the main comparison, the runs as first
specified and the post hoc check in the appendix, and Laraib's and Rhea's independent checks of
the discussion and of every number folded in.

\textbf{Still deferred:} cluster annotation with a language model, the hub correction for the
mutual information graph, and GPU work, and from the Assignment 2 selection plan the
significance rule in the style of sc-SHC, the runs on 2,000 variable genes and the label-free
runs on the Allen subset, set aside so that one rule could be completed on every dataset first.
We will ask the instructor whether any of these belongs in the course project before starting
one.

\textbf{The full atlas.} Our 100,000-cell pilot of MICA as shipped took 7.1 hours and peaked
at 140 GB, which scales to about 6 to 8 days and 3.2 TB for all 2.3 million cells, against the
754 GB on our machine. A faster graph does not close that gap. Our shortlist build alone
peaked at 80 GB on the same cells, about 1.8 TB in proportion, so the whole atlas would need a
sketch, clustering a sample and assigning the rest. We will not change the course-scale plan
for the atlas without first discussing it with the instructor.
