\section{Conclusion and Remaining Work}\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked whether the advantage scMINER reports for mutual information clustering survives
when the method must choose its own clustering without reference labels. Applying one
published label-free rule identically to MICA and to a principal component and Leiden
pipeline, on nine datasets with repeats, we find that the ranking depends on how the rule
resolves settings it cannot distinguish, and that it cannot distinguish a wide span of
cluster counts for MICA. The published benchmark score is therefore not a quantity a user
inherits, and the uncertainty introduced by the selection rule is larger on these datasets
than the difference between the two methods.

Three pieces of work remain before the complete draft on November 18, and they are chosen
to address the limitations above rather than to add scope.

First, PBMC14K under MICA's graph mode. It is the only dataset in our set above the 5,000
cell threshold and the only one whose labels do not come from clustering, so it tests
whether the ambiguity we measured is a property of small datasets or of the method. The run
is implemented and queued.

Second, a transfer rule, in which each dataset receives the setting that worked best on the
others. It is the remedy Ullmann et al. recommend for exactly the bias we are studying, and
it also offers a way to check whether MICA's shipped default carries information from these
benchmark datasets.

Third, the scaling result from Assignment 2 needs its cost argument completed rather
than extended. We already match MICA's own neighbour search accuracy at a fraction of
its cost on 13,605 and 100,000 cells, but what that is worth depends on whether the
resulting clustering is one a user would have selected, which is the question this
submission has been asking. We will not add the retrieval and language model component
unless the clustering results are settled first.
