\section{Conclusion and Remaining Work}\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked whether the advantage scMINER reports for mutual information clustering survives
when the method must choose its own clustering without reference labels. Applying one
published label-free rule identically to MICA and to a principal component and Leiden
pipeline, on ten datasets with repeats, we find that on the nine small ones the ranking
depends on how the rule resolves settings it cannot distinguish, and that it cannot
distinguish a wide span of cluster counts for MICA. On the tenth, the only one above 5,000
cells and the only one whose labels do not come from clustering, the ambiguity is absent
and MICA wins outright. Running both of MICA's modes on one fixed pool of 2,500 cells
locates the cause in the pipeline rather than the dataset size. The published benchmark
score is therefore not a quantity a user inherits in MICA's small-data mode, where nine of
the ten benchmark datasets sit, and the uncertainty the selection rule introduces there is
larger than the difference between the two methods.

One experiment and one piece of analysis remain, for the main experiment deadline of
November 4 and the complete draft on November 18. They address the limitations above rather
than add scope. The experiment is a transfer rule, in which each dataset receives the
setting that worked best on the others. It is the remedy Ullmann et al. recommend for the
bias we are studying, and it checks whether MICA's shipped default carries information from
these benchmark datasets. The analysis is the Assignment 2 scaling result, which needs its
cost argument completed rather than extended: we match MICA's neighbour search accuracy at
a fraction of its cost on 13,605 and 100,000 cells, but what that is worth depends on
whether the resulting clustering is one a user would have selected. We will not add the
retrieval and language model component unless the clustering results settle first.
