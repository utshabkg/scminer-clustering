\section{Conclusion and Remaining Work}\label{sec:conclusion}
When one label-free rule chooses the setting for every method, the ranking it produces depends on more
than the methods. On the scMINER benchmark the candidates offered to each method decided the
plain-maximum ranking and reversed it on the nine small datasets' original grids, and the reading of the
scores and the number of subsamples each moved it further, while the bootstrap interval moved individual
choices. The effects of the candidates and of the reading held at seven new seeds against criteria fixed in advance. The effects of the number of subsamples and of the interval were first measured in these runs, and the protocol gave them no verdict. chooseR's own code has only the finest-stable reading, so it could test only the interval. Between the methods, MICA's lead under
chooseR's reading on matched candidates held at every seed, under our scorer and under chooseR's code with
either declared repair, but the datasets split about evenly and with 25 subsamples the mean lead rests on
one of them. PCA plus $k$-means, the textbook baseline, scored lowest in every design on the nine small datasets, and with the menu and the clustering step held fixed MICA led it by \V{E2.mid.F.micaminuskmeans} under chooseR's reading. On PBMC14K, MICA and Leiden tie on matched candidates under the plain maximum at four of five seeds, but the tie depends on which setting stands for each count, and on the original grids MICA leads at every seed. A comparison of clustering methods under label-free
selection should therefore match the candidate counts and report the setting behind each, check that the scores are not flat at the top, and check that the ranking holds under both readings, across draws, across implementations of the rule and without each dataset.

\subsection*{Plan through November 30}
\textbf{The large-scale dataset.} The course requires one of at least two million observations. We will use
the Allen whole mouse brain atlas, 2.3 million cells \cite{yao2023allen}, and report its dimensions after
preprocessing. Label-free selection by subsampling is out of reach for MICA at that size. Our Assignment 2
pilot of MICA as shipped took 7.1 hours and 140 GB on 100,000 of these cells, which scales to about 6 to 8
days and 3.2 TB for the whole atlas, against the 754 GB on our machine. We will therefore run PCA with
$k$-means and with Leiden on all cells, and MICA on a sample with the remaining cells assigned to its
clusters, and apply the stability rule to subsamples of a size all three methods can run.

\textbf{The size test.} Drawing pools of 150 and 300 cells from Zeisel and from PBMC14K and rerunning the
matched rule tests whether MICA's flat top comes from small samples or from the datasets themselves.

\textbf{Still set aside:} the significance rule, the runs on 2,000 variable genes, the label-free runs on the
Allen subset, annotation with a language model, the hub correction and GPU work.
