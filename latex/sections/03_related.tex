\section{Closest Work}\label{sec:related}
Four bodies of work bear on our question. The first three lead to the measurement we make, and the
fourth to our graph comparison.

\textbf{The method under test.} scMINER \cite{pan2025scminer} introduces MICA and reports a mean
adjusted Rand index (ARI) of 0.84 across ten labelled datasets, against 0.73 for Seurat and 0.68
for Scanpy. Every setting in that benchmark is fixed with the labels, and all ten headline scores come from MICA at the true number of clusters. Asked during peer review how a user should choose the resolution without
labels, the authors pointed to silhouette scores and marker genes but tested neither. We found no
independent benchmark that includes MICA.

\textbf{Choosing a clustering without labels.} chooseR \cite{pattersoncross2021chooser} is the rule
we adopt. It reruns a pipeline on 100 random 80 percent subsamples per candidate setting, scores
each setting by how reliably cells co-cluster, and keeps the finest setting that stays above a
threshold derived from the settings themselves. Its authors note that this leans toward mild
overclustering. We found no application of it to a mutual information method. Yu et al.
\cite{yu2022benchmarking} benchmarked 14 methods that estimate the number of cell types, finding that
most underestimate it and that a good ARI did not imply a correct count, which is why we report the
chosen count beside the score. A second family, sc-SHC \cite{grabski2023significance} and CHOIR
\cite{sant2025choir}, asks whether a split is real rather than merely reproducible. None of these
covers a mutual information method, and the distinction matters here, because a partition can be
perfectly reproducible without being real.

\textbf{Tuning on the ground truth.} Ullmann et al. \cite{ullmann2023overoptimistic} tuned a
published clustering algorithm against known labels to make it look good, then showed how much of
that advantage disappeared when the choices moved. They used toy and UCI data and one algorithm, with
no single-cell data and no label-free rule tested as the remedy. A 2025 preprint
\cite{szmigiel2025benchmarking} swept Leiden and Louvain on 16 single-cell datasets and found that
on six the Scanpy default did no better than a typical random setting. These studies establish the
general concern. None measures it for MICA, whose published scores were all obtained with
label-informed settings. We supply that measurement, and with it something the general concern does
not predict: the effect depends on which cluster counts each method's grid can offer.

\textbf{Scaling the neighbour graph.} MICA's graph mode finds neighbours with the HNSW graph index
\cite{malkov2020efficient}. Proposing candidates cheaply and rescoring them exactly is the standard
alternative, as in the refinement step of Faiss \cite{douze2024faiss}, and like any shortlist it
finds only the true neighbours that land in it. Song et al. \cite{song2012comparison} found that
binned mutual information tracks correlation closely for gene pairs, which suggested correlation as
the cheap stage.
