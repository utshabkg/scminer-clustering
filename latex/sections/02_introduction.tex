\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
A biologist who has sequenced a new tissue wants to know which cell types are in it. The
standard route is to cluster the cells and inspect the groups, and every method used for this
has a setting that controls how finely it divides the data. On Zeisel, which has 7 annotated
cell types, a Leiden pipeline returns 3 or 4 clusters at a resolution of 0.05, depending on the seed,
and 21 at 1.0, the setting its software ships with. That choice has to be made without knowing
the answer, because the number of cell types is usually what the experiment is trying to
establish.

Published benchmarks do not make that decision. In the benchmark for scMINER
\cite{pan2025scminer}, the framework we study, every method is either given the true
number of cell types or has its setting swept and keeps only the runs reproducing that
number, with ties broken by agreement with the labels.
Ullmann et al. \cite{ullmann2023overoptimistic} show that tuning against the ground
truth inflates a method's apparent advantage. What is missing is a direct measurement of
what happens to a specific published advantage when the selection is made without labels.

\textbf{Main question.} When the same label-free rule chooses the setting for both methods,
does MICA, the mutual information clustering in scMINER, produce partitions closer to the
known cell types than a principal component and Leiden pipeline on the same genes? Two
supporting questions follow. How much of the answer is set by the rule rather than by either
method, and under what conditions does the answer hold?

Our approach holds the data and the rule fixed and varies the method. The main rule is
subsampling stability in the style of chooseR \cite{pattersoncross2021chooser}, which reruns each
method on random 80 percent subsamples and prefers settings whose clusters survive resampling. As
first specified, both methods chose among settings yielding at most 20 clusters. In the runs we
rely on, both are offered the same cluster counts, one setting per count. Labels are used only
afterwards, to score what the rule chose. The experiment covers all ten benchmark datasets at three
seeds, with cost recorded.

As first specified, MICA leads under three of four rules,
but its mean moves by 0.255 across them against 0.120 for the baseline, and two readings of one rule
reverse the ranking. The cause is a confound: a cap does not give the methods the same candidates.
On matched counts the reversal is gone and MICA leads under both readings at every seed, because the
baseline's score falls once its grid reaches the coarse partitions MICA's always could. MICA's
stability scores still sit flat at the top, mostly on the smallest datasets, and a label-informed
transfer shows that its default of four clusters is what tuning on this benchmark would pick. On
PBMC14K, the one large benchmark dataset, the ranking turns on the rule's reading and threshold.



\textbf{A measurement of what label-free selection does to a published advantage.} The closest
work establishes that tuning on labels is optimistic \cite{ullmann2023overoptimistic} and supplies
rules meant to replace it \cite{pattersoncross2021chooser,liu2021multik}, but we found none applied
to mutual information clustering, and the scMINER benchmark reports no label-free result. We find
that the tie-break and the subsample draw inside one rule each move the result by as much as the gap
between the methods, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} turns this into what a user should do
differently.

\textbf{Evidence that a cap is not a match.} The reversal came from counts one method could reach
and the other could not, and it disappears when both are offered the same counts. The ties that
remain are largely MICA's, and the rule's two readings break them in opposite directions.

\textbf{A cost and quality comparison of a faster neighbour graph.} A correlation shortlist rescored
with the exact MICA distance builds the graph faster with no worse recall. It is not guaranteed to
find every true neighbour, it needs three to four times the memory to build, and it gave no better clustering.

Sections~\ref{sec:related} to~\ref{sec:design} cover the closest work, the methods and the design,
Section~\ref{sec:results} the results, and Sections~\ref{sec:discussion} and~\ref{sec:conclusion} what
follows from them and what remains.
